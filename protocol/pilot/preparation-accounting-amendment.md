# Prospective preparation accounting for cases 02–08

**Status:** Coordinator instruction recorded 17 November 2026; prospective operational interpretation for case-specific qualification and lock. It does not authorize a case, alter the original limits, or change the locked PILOT-01 primary or its ledger. Independent human review remains pending.

Common source, runtime and dependency artifact preparation takes place before the timed attempt. Its historical wall time and transfer volume are unknown where receipts are missing; neither is represented as zero or as measured. This boundary does not treat preparation as completed isolated installation. Before each case lock, inventory the exact retained staged inputs and make a conservative, once-only staged-input byte predebit against the original 20 GiB case application-byte allowance at first ledger initialization. This is a conservative accounting allocation based on file lengths, **not a measurement of the earlier network transfer**. Record the inventory, any duplicate copies counted, the predebit, and its application to the ledger. Reopening or resuming the case must not debit the same frozen inputs twice. Later input changes require a recorded, separately counted amendment.

The six-hour elapsed clock starts at actual isolated setup. Isolated dependency installation, candidate execution and independent reference work consume the same original case resource meters; their actual elapsed and resource use must be recorded. Setup failures and blocked work remain outcomes and do not reset the ledger. The broker counts application request and response bytes rather than all wire traffic; neither the predebit nor this meter establishes a 20 GiB total lifecycle wire-traffic cap.

The [provisional per-case input inventory](../../evidence/executor/prospective-case-resource-accounting.json) lists known files and hashes. It is not a final inventory, evidence that the predebits have been applied, or a substitute for case-specific qualification. The 52 CRAN source archives streamed during common exploratory preparation are reported as a shared pool; their total is not assigned as an observed per-case transfer. Historical metadata downloads, duplicate requests, protocol overhead and preparation time were not fully recorded.

For R cases, the previously proposed 48 GiB workload image, 48 GiB retained outputs and 4 GiB runtime/control allocation leaves a 3 GiB runtime slice that may not fit both the approximately 2.8 GiB R tree and approximately 2.5 GiB host runtime. A **46 GiB workload image, 46 GiB retained outputs and 8 GiB runtime/control allocation** is proposed as a conservative alternative within 100 GiB. Measure the actual frozen copies and bounded control writers and implement and test the chosen case-specific ceilings before claiming this allocation is enforced. The existing case 01 allocation and records remain unchanged.
