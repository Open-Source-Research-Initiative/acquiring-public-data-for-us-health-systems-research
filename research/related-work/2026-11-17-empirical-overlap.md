# Empirical overlap: source acquisition and source-to-output checks

Exploratory search, 17 November 2026 (UTC). This is a bounded contribution check, not a systematic review or evidence that comparable studies do not exist. It uses five recorded web queries, follow-up lookups for source metadata, and selective reading of primary sources. Full queries, access status and inspection limits are in [the search ledger](2026-11-17-empirical-overlap.json). No software was executed and no individual-level data were accessed. A human review of search, source interpretation, and study implications is pending.

## Most important overlap

[Wykrota and Kęczkowska, 2026](https://doi.org/10.3390/app16178454) assess their own harmonization framework on public hospital discharge data in five jurisdictions including New York State. The paper describes 99 input files and a 14-field model, versioned mappings, source-profile checks, value-domain tests, row and information-loss auditing, and a field-level audit prepared from provider documentation. For one source the authors compare output counts and a length-of-stay measure to independently published provider summaries. They also report a discrepancy for one year as unresolved and explicitly state that a second analyst has not independently replicated a crosswalk. Some source files were retained in canonical layout rather than as provider exports, limiting original-source verification. Publisher full-text sections inspected: abstract, methods/results extracts, discussion and limitations; supplements and code were not independently inspected. These are authors' reported results, not independently replicated here.

This is a substantive challenge to any claim that source-referenced preservation checks for public healthcare data are new. Its public record-level discharge data are outside the proposed non-patient-level population, and it evaluates one author-developed harmonization framework rather than independently sampling and executing separate third-party acquisition workflows. Its provider-summary comparisons and source-documentation checks are meaningful external references, but are not evidence of an independent analyst rerunning each separate workflow or of the proposed release-identity test. These distinctions need confirmation for each detailed claim, rather than treating scope differences as proof of novelty.

## Contribution or replication justification

A defensible prospective question is whether independent, document-guided users of a defined sample of publicly inspectable non-patient-level US health-systems acquisition workflows can verify both the intended publisher release or comparable snapshot and preservation of explicitly chosen source facts in generated output. The added result would be the observed distribution of verified, contradicted and unresolved outcomes under the stated selection, resource and evidence rules, plus case-level traceability. It is a particular independent evaluation of an acquisition-workflow population and not an invention of data provenance, public-health pipeline execution, source-to-target checking or output comparison.

If another study is found that already performs materially equivalent independent source-release and preservation checks across the same kind of workflows, revise the contribution or explicitly justify replication by a prespecified difference in population, source-release period or attempt conditions. Do not assert novelty by lack of discovery. Results cannot support general research fitness, publisher data truth, completeness across all rows, or continuing reliability. For acquisition identity, the task must specify a verifiable version or comparable snapshot and an independent source reference; if either is lacking, report unresolved rather than a failure or success. For transformed facts, an independent assessor must predefine source records, rules, and a comparison oracle without exposing expected results to the executing agent before output lock.

Prior planning already identified Samuel and Mietchen's publication-linked biomedical notebook reruns and output comparison, Grayson et al.'s workflow registry execution study, the 2026 provider-reporting scoping review, and DATA-CARE. Those earlier checks are not counted as new inspections in this search. The newly identified 2026 harmonization evaluation is the closest empirical overlap in this pass and should be added to any related-work section before committing the main study. Follow source and citing-paper trails in a protocolized search before making a manuscript-level contribution claim.
