## Closest empirical and review work

The newly rechecked [Wykrota and Kęczkowska paper](https://doi.org/10.3390/app16178454) reports one author-built harmonization framework for five public hospital-discharge data sources, including New York. Its source-documentation-based field audit and comparisons with publisher summaries already establish source-referenced checks in public health-systems data. The authors explicitly report no second-analyst replication of their crosswalk. The study uses record-level discharge data, outside the present non-patient-level scope, and is not shown in inspected sections to independently sample and execute multiple third-party acquisition systems. Inspect the publisher version history and supplement before relying on detailed numbers. Evidence details are recorded in [the earlier empirical search](../related-work/2026-11-17-empirical-overlap.md); its observations were re-examined as a cited comparison, not a new workflow experiment.

[Samuel and Mietchen](https://doi.org/10.1093/gigascience/giad113) independently reran publication-linked biomedical notebooks and compared original and regenerated outputs. [Grayson and colleagues](https://doi.org/10.1145/3589806.3600037) assessed non-crashing executions and workflow revisions in two registries. Those methods defeat a novelty claim for independent biomedical workflow execution or output equality. Their populations and inspected outcomes are distinct from the proposed independent publisher-release and bounded source-fact check; identity of output alone does not determine the correctness of its upstream source. The [2025 government-health-data review](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12889-024-21149-6) and [2026 provider-reporting review](https://doi.org/10.2196/84820) already review related data and reporting infrastructure; a systematic map alone should not be presented as an established research gap.

## Verdict for feasibility and the pilot

**Research question:** partly occupied. Public-data acquisition demonstrations, source-to-target validation, and cross-pipeline comparisons exist. **Frame:** substantial conceptual overlap, exact overlap unresolved; the public non-patient-level US acquisition-workflow frame remains a specified study choice. **Measurement:** release identity plus independent source-derived, record/field-level preservation checks across sampled third-party workflows is a proposed combination, not confirmed unique. **Journal-specific crowding:** not assessed; no target-journal novelty claim is supported.

Proceed with reference and pilot preparation as feasibility work. The useful result would be case-level evidence of whether selected workflows obtained independently verifiable intended releases or snapshots and preserved a prespecified, bounded set of source-defined facts; report inaccessible and unassessable cases alongside successes and discrepancies. This supports selection and verification decisions for the examined workflows, not correctness of publisher data, full dataset completeness, research fitness or maintenance performance. The map provides an explicit sampling frame even if it overlaps an existing inventory.

Before any main evaluation is proposed, complete the study's existing protocol gates: freeze separate execution and assessment packets, obtain independent qualified human review, and demonstrate the required actual assessable comparisons across source families. Revisit this assessment when the exact review corpus or a materially equivalent independent evaluation becomes available. If equivalent work already answers the same question in the same frame, require a concrete new population, release period or method, or an explicitly justified replication; otherwise redesign. A failed pilot, missing reference, or inaccessible source is not evidence that a workflow is incorrect. No candidate workflow was executed, no public health payload downloaded, and no remote repository changed in this assessment.

## Focused audit of the public hospital-data harmonization paper

On 17 November 2026, the [publisher article](https://www.mdpi.com/2076-3417/16/17/8454), its supplementary-material description and [Zenodo software-record metadata](https://zenodo.org/api/records/21982702) were inspected without opening health data or running code. This is a closer overlap than a generic ETL quality example: the authors describe a record-level audit that returns to provider files and derives selected values from provider documentation without invoking the evaluated crosswalk engine. They report that it exposed a New York monetary conversion issue and prompted a crosswalk revision. They also compared transformed results with independently published provider aggregates for one source. The article says validation and software were performed by the same author and explicitly reports that no second analyst independently replicated a crosswalk. Thus the external provider evidence is distinct from the framework, but the assessment itself is not demonstrated to have been independently conducted by an external executor.

The article describes source-specific vintage, provider documentation and access records; the software metadata describes a pipeline that downloads selected inputs, mainly from Brazil. Its supplement description lists source access records, vintage-specific documentation, a validation protocol and mappings. These are substantive acquisition and provenance precedents. The inspected text and metadata do **not demonstrate** that the study independently checked the intended publisher release or comparable snapshot of every automated acquisition, using a separate assessor and fixed task; they do not establish that such checks were absent. Some Korean and Brazilian files were not retained as first-hand provider exports, limiting original-source verification for those cases, as the authors state. This study's source-record comparisons therefore substantially narrow any claim of a new external source oracle; the proposed empirical distinction is the combined sampling frame, independently conducted document-guided acquisition attempts, prelocked task and reference separation, and explicit release/snapshot disposition—not source-to-output checks alone.

The publisher's supplementary-file description and availability statement identify documentation, access records, validation scripts and crosswalks. The Zenodo API describes the deposit as open MIT-licensed software and lists a single ZIP; its files were not downloaded or inspected, and the metadata description is an author claim rather than verified executable behavior. The publisher's version-notes link returned HTTP 429 in the web interface and HTTP 403 via direct request; the actual version history was not accessible. The article itself mentions a crosswalk revision and a superseded submitted-version figure; these do not establish the publisher's full article-version history. The supplementary-file link produced a decoding error in the web interface, so its actual tables were not inspected. Recheck version notes and the supplement before relying on precise version-specific measurements. This audit does not establish novelty or independent replication.
